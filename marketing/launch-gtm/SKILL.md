# Launch and Go-to-Market

The discipline of getting a product in front of its first users and turning a launch moment into durable demand. It covers channel selection for a launch, the Product Hunt machine, earned media, scarcity mechanics, and naming so attention lands on you instead of a rival.

## When to use this

Reach for this when there is a moment to capitalize on or a cold start to solve:

- Launching a new product or feature and choosing how to get the first users.
- Planning a Product Hunt launch (timeline, assets, hunter, ranking).
- Chasing press, editor relationships, or lower CAC by borrowing publication credibility.
- Running a limited drop, cohort, or capped offer that needs real urgency.
- Standing out in a discount-heavy window (BFCM) with a campaign rather than a promo.
- Naming a product, or running non-clickable media (billboards, radio, podcasts, TV) where recall is the whole game.

Symptoms that point here: "we're launching next month and have no plan", "our billboard sent traffic to a competitor", "how do we sell out 30 seats", "how do we get on TechCrunch".

## When NOT to use this (reach for instead)

- **To decide IF a channel is your durable growth engine** (not just the launch spike), use `strategy-fundamentals`. This skill picks the first-users motion; that one picks the channels you compound on.
- **To actually run and scale paid ads** behind a launch or an earned mention, use `paid-acquisition`. This skill tells you when to put spend behind PR; that one runs the accounts.
- **To build the ongoing content/community/social engine** the launch hands off to, use `organic-social`.
- **For the words inside the ad, landing page, or maker comment**, pair with a copywriting skill if present.

## How this works (decision path)

1. **Start with the channel question.** Before copying anyone's launch, place yourself on two axes: purchase intent (are buyers actively searching?) and market conditions. Use the GTM play picker to choose 2 to 3 of the 12 named plays. This routes everything below.
2. **If a launch platform is one of your plays,** run the Product Hunt track: the launch is won in the 3 to 4 weeks before launch day, so start the timeline early, then apply the ranking mechanics (quality-weighted vote velocity, hunter choice, community warm-up).
3. **If press is a play,** treat PR as a performance channel: target specific editors, build journalist relationships instead of cold-pitching, and put paid spend behind the mentions you earn to compound them.
4. **If the launch is a limited/paid offer,** manufacture real scarcity with the lightest possible stack (Google Doc + Stripe link + live seat count). Cap supply so the scarcity is genuine.
5. **If the moment calls for a brand swing,** build a standalone creative campaign (a focused microsite or a purpose-driven anti-sale) that earns organic attention a product page never could.
6. **Underneath all of it, get the name right.** People recall the category term, not your clever brand, so name for the searched keyword and bid on it behind every offline campaign, or you send buyers to a rival.

## Key numbers and benchmarks

- **Product Hunt outcome:** bottom of front page = 2,000+ visitors; #1 = 10,000+ visitors, converting to roughly 1,000 to 3,000 genuine leads.
- **Product Hunt KPI:** optimize for email capture (target 1,000+ emails), not raw upvotes.
- **Product Hunt prerequisites:** activate an audience of at least 400 people; build the list 3 to 4 weeks out.
- **Product Hunt timing:** post at 12:01am PST, Tuesday to Thursday only. Ranking weights vote velocity and account age; a flood of brand-new accounts voting for you is a demotion signal. Ask for feedback, not upvotes.
- **Product Hunt assets:** thumbnail 240x240px animated GIF; gallery images 635x380px, strongest first.

- **Creative campaign:** a ~2-hour interactive microsite drew 100,000 organic users; 94% of consumers say they value companies with a strong sense of purpose.
- **Scarcity drop:** one capped Cyber Monday offer sold out 30 seats in one hour by showing "90 viewers vs 30 spots".

### Launch a standalone creative campaign, a focused microsite on its own domain, or a purpose-driven anti-sale, to earn organic attention

## The strategy
A focused, interactive one-off experience, separate from your cluttered main site, can pull large organic traffic that a product page never would. And during discount-heavy moments (BFCM), a purpose-driven campaign can stand out more than any promo.

## When to use it
A launch moment, brand-awareness push, or a period (like Black Friday) where everyone else is running the same discount playbook.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Build a campaign microsite on a separate domain (issue #053):** focused, minimalist, interactive, and linked back to the main brand, Calm "Do Nothing for 2 Minutes," Adobe Creative Types, IKilledZoom.com.
2. **Keep it tiny and shippable:** Digiday's took ~2 hours to build and drew 100,000 organic users.
3. **Make it inherently shareable:** an interactive toy, a self-test, or a bold statement people forward.
4. **Or run a purpose-driven alternative to discounts (issue #091):** REI #OptOutside (closed on Black Friday), Allbirds raised prices and donated the difference. "94% of consumers value companies with a strong sense of purpose."

## Notes / caveats / examples
- The microsite wins because it's unburdened by nav, conversion widgets, and brand baggage, a single clear interaction.
- Purpose-driven campaigns work precisely because they zag while competitors all zig on price.

## → Skill conversion note
Lower skill value (a campaign-ideation prompt); could become a "campaign concept generator" that proposes an interactive microsite hook or a purpose-driven anti-sale angle for a brand and moment.

### Earn press with "performance PR" and journalist relationships, put ad spend behind mentions, and befriend reporters instead of cold-pitching

## The strategy
Treat PR as a performance channel, not a spray-and-pray press release. Get in front of the specific editors you want, amplify the mentions you earn with paid spend (they're cheap and convert), and build real relationships with journalists so you become their go-to source.

## When to use it
Launching, seeking tier-1 press, or trying to lower CAC by borrowing publication credibility.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Run performance PR (issue #062):** run ads targeting industry editors before outreach; join affiliate platforms (Impact, Skimlinks, ShareASale) to earn mentions; and put ad spend behind existing media mentions to compound their reach.
2. **Amplify earned mentions:** a single strong article, boosted with paid, can reset your unit economics.
3. **Befriend journalists, don't pitch them (issue #114):** find their emails with Hunter or Voila Norbert, open a genuine conversation, offer to be a future source, and respond fast. HARO went spammy, relationships beat pitches.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Hint Water: after a Hustle article, CPC fell ~$5 → <10¢ and CAC dropped $80 → $25, because they put paid spend behind the earned mention.
- The relationship play is a long game: being a reliable, fast source gets you into stories cold pitches never would.

## → Skill conversion note
Lower skill value (a PR-process guide); could become a "performance PR planner" that builds an editor target list, drafts a relationship-first outreach message, and plans paid amplification of earned mentions.

### Pick your first-users GTM play off two axes, purchase intent × market conditions, from 12 named plays on a flowchart

## The strategy
There's no universal launch channel, the right first-users play depends on whether your buyers are actively searching (purchase intent) and on your market conditions. Map 12 concrete plays onto flowcharts so you pick by situation, not by imitation.

## When to use it
Getting a new product's first users; choosing a go-to-market motion for a launch.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Place yourself on two axes:** purchase intent (high/low) × market conditions.
2. **Pick from the 12 named plays** sorted onto the flowcharts: discoverable content, overservice one customer, hack distribution, forum fishing, cold outreach + friction reduction, launch platforms/PR, warm outreach, embed in communities, street-level attention, build in public, influencer partnerships, full PR.
3. **Match play to intent:** high-intent/searching buyers → discoverable content, launch platforms, cold outreach; low-intent buyers → community embedding, build in public, influencer partnerships, street-level attention.
4. **For geographically constrained products, go street-level:** Honeypot placed whiteboards around London to reach a local developer audience.

## Notes / caveats / examples

- "Overservice one customer" and "forum fishing" are the underrated manual plays most launches skip.

### Sell a limited launch with a Google Doc + Stripe link + live-updated seat counts to manufacture real scarcity and FOMO

## The strategy
You don't need a polished sales page or checkout system to sell out a limited offer. A plain Google Doc, a Stripe payment link, and a manually updated seat count create genuine, visible scarcity that drives fast FOMO-fueled purchases.

## When to use it
Selling a cohort program, limited service, workshop, or capped product, especially early, before you've built real infrastructure.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Write the offer in a Google Doc**, description, price, and terms in plain text.
2. **Drop in a Stripe payment link** for instant purchase.
3. **Show live-updated seat counts / viewer numbers** to make scarcity visible (manual updates are fine).
4. **Cap supply tightly** so the scarcity is real, not theatrical.
5. **Announce to your warm audience** and let the shrinking seat count do the selling.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- One capped Cyber Monday drop sold out 30 seats in one hour with this exact setup; showing "90 viewers vs 30 spots" drove the FOMO.
- The unpolished format is a feature, it signals authenticity and urgency over a slick funnel.

## → Skill conversion note
Lower skill value (a lightweight launch mechanic); could become a template that generates the Google Doc offer copy + a scarcity-counter structure for a capped launch.

### Product Hunt launch, pre-launch timeline, timing, and assets

## The strategy
A Product Hunt launch is won in the 3-4 weeks before launch day. Optimize for email capture (KPI: 1,000+ emails), not upvotes, and set up assets + timing precisely. Realistic outcome: bottom of front page = 2,000+ visitors; #1 = 10,000+ visitors → 1,000-3,000 genuine leads.

## When to use it
Launching a product with at least a 400-person audience to activate.

## How to execute (steps)
- Pre-launch (3-4 weeks out): build 400+ people minimum; email your list explaining PH's value and asking them to create accounts (only a small % engage, normal).
- Warm-up (3 days before): email asking people to upvote/comment on OTHER existing PH products, so their accounts look active to the algorithm.
- Launch timing: post at 12:01am PST (PH runs on PST regardless of your location) on a Tuesday, Thursday only (avoid Monday catch-up and Friday tune-out).
- Launch day: send email batches throughout the day (not one blast) to maintain vote velocity; ask for FEEDBACK, not upvotes (upvote requests can trigger demotion).
- Assets: thumbnail 240x240px animated GIF (motion beats static logos); gallery images 635x380px with the strongest first (used for social sharing); title = product name only, no emoji/description; use a personal (not business) account; validate with the Preview Hunt tool and the "would I click this myself?" test.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Bonus: top 5 products get featured in the next day's PH digest email.
- Maker comment: introduce yourself/team + problem; break into Problem (why) / Solution (how) / Monetization; humble and helpful comments attract more comments; consider a PH-specific landing page offer using ?ref=producthunt.

## → Skill conversion note
A "PH launch planner" skill: generate a dated 4-week countdown checklist, email-batch schedule, asset spec sheet, and maker-comment draft.

### Product Hunt ranking mechanics, hunter strategy, and communities

## The strategy
PH ranks on quality-weighted vote velocity, not raw upvote count. Upvotes from established, months-old accounts count far more; a flood of brand-new accounts voting for you is a demotion signal. Choose your hunter and pre-build social capital accordingly.

## How to execute (steps)
- Understand the algorithm: upvotes from long-standing PH members are weighted higher (account age = authenticity proxy); products with fewer high-quality upvotes can outrank high-volume ones; new accounts voting for you trigger demotion.
- Hunter choice, Option A: get a superuser to hunt (find top hunters on the Upvote Bell leaderboard, e.g. Chris Messina, Hiten Shah) for auto front-page placement + social proof. Option B: self-hunt (PH-approved) if you have strong pre-launch momentum.
- Build social capital in the weeks before by contributing genuine value in relevant communities: Slack (product-school, startupstudygroup, launch-chat, Growthhackertalk), Facebook groups (producthuntupvoters, producthunt.exchange), Indie Hackers, Startup School, give value before asking for support.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Because velocity + account quality matter more than volume, buying/spamming upvotes backfires.

## → Skill conversion note
A "PH ranking optimizer" skill: given launch assets and audience, output a hunter recommendation, a community-warm-up plan, and vote-velocity guidance that avoids demotion triggers.

### Name for the keyword people will actually search, and bid on that remembered term behind every offline/brand ad, or you'll send traffic to a rival

## The strategy
People don't remember your clever product name from a billboard or podcast ad, they remember the category term. If you don't own that remembered keyword in search, your offline and brand spend sends buyers straight to a competitor.

## When to use it
Naming a product, or running non-immediate-action media (billboards, radio, podcasts, TV) where the audience can't click.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Name/domain for the keyword users will actually search,** i.e. the simplified category term they'll type later.
2. **Bid Google Ads on that remembered keyword** behind every offline/brand campaign, so the recalled search lands on you, not a rival.
3. **Enable direct action** in the ad itself where possible, a phone number, a familiar iOS-notification mockup, to reduce the search step.
4. **Use ruthless whitespace** so the one memorable term/action dominates.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Qualified's "Piper" billboard sent traffic to rival AiSDR because people recalled the category term "AI SDR," not the brand mascot.
- A NYC restaurant capturing "Thai Food Near Me" is the same idea at local scale, own the term people actually type.

## → Skill conversion note
Lower skill value (a naming/bidding heuristic); could become a checklist that tests a product name against the category term people will search and generates the defensive branded/keyword ad plan for offline campaigns.
