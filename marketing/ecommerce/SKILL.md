# Ecommerce and DTC Growth

Grow a physical-goods or consumable store by fixing the surfaces that decide the sale:
the storefront, the cart, the order value, the promo framing, the marketplace listing,
and shipping. The store-wide formula is the same one CRO uses: **Conversion = Desire
minus Labor minus Confusion**. The difference here is the ecommerce-specific machinery
built on top of it: bundles, promos, scarcity, marketplaces, and reorder loops.

## When to use this

- Launching or auditing a Shopify / DTC storefront (homepage, product pages, checkout).
- Carts are abandoned at a measurable rate between add-to-cart and purchase.
- Average order value is flat and cross-sells or bundles are not moving it.
- Planning a sale or peak season and the discount alone isn't cutting through.
- Selling on Amazon and ad spend is high while organic rank and conversion stall.
- Shipping cost or unclear delivery timing is killing conversion.
- A consumable product has weak repeat-purchase rates.

## When NOT to use this (reach for instead)

- **Page-level conversion mechanics** (the hero formula, checkout field surgery, review
  display science, popup timing, the belief-level funnel audit): `conversion-cro`. This
  skill points there for the on-page work and adds the store-and-catalog layer on top.
- **Keeping customers and driving repeat revenue over time** (lifecycle email flows,
  subscriptions as a program, churn, win-back): `retention-lifecycle`. This skill covers
  the reorder-at-the-moment loop and the shipping membership; the lifecycle program lives
  there.
- **Bringing traffic to the store in the first place** (Meta/Google/TikTok ads, cost per
  acquisition, ROAS, creative): `paid-acquisition`.

## How this works (decision path)

1. **Get the storefront converting first.** Ads and promos amplify a good store and waste
   money on a bad one. Audit homepage, PDP, and checkout against Desire minus Labor minus
   Confusion (`shopify-store-conversion`). Average Shopify conversion is 1.6%; the top 10%
   hit 5.1%+, and the gap is copy, imagery, and flow, not the theme.
2. **Plug the cart leak.** 70 to 80% of carts are abandoned, and most of it traces to five
   predictable frictions led by shipping cost. Attack each, then recover the rest by email
   (`cart-abandonment-reduction`).
3. **Win the shipping objection specifically.** Show delivery dates on the PDP before the
   cart, and consider a Prime-style free-shipping membership for frequent buyers
   (`shipping-and-delivery-conversion`).
4. **Raise order value with tested bundles.** Build bundles from real co-purchase data,
   validate on unpaid channels, and only discount the proven winner (`bundles-and-aov`).
5. **Frame promos better, not deeper.** The same discount converts more as a "leak," a
   curiosity sequence, or an ownable obscure holiday (`promotional-framing-tactics`).
6. **Use scarcity as a signal.** Keep 10 to 30% of the catalog visibly sold-out for social
   proof and urgency, and stay under the 30% choice-threat ceiling (`sold-out-scarcity`).
7. **Fix the marketplace lane.** On Amazon, optimize the listing for performance and
   relevance before scaling PPC, then mine PPC data for backend keywords
   (`amazon-listing-optimization`).
8. **Close the reorder loop.** For consumables, print a QR reorder code on the packaging
   to capture repurchase at the moment the product runs out (`qr-reorder-codes`).

## Key numbers and benchmarks

- **Shopify conversion:** average 1.6%; top 20% hit 3.5%+; top 10% hit 5.1%+. The gap is
  copy, imagery, and flow, not the theme.
- **Cart abandonment:** 70 to 80% of carts abandoned. Top 5 causes, ranked: extra/shipping
  cost, forced account creation, slow delivery, long checkout, card-trust concerns.
- **Shipping:** 48% of US shoppers abandon over shipping cost. A free-shipping
  subscription lifts revenue per customer ~34%.
- **Cross-sells:** 52% of sites recommend irrelevant products and actively hurt AOV.
  Relevance, not quantity, drives attach rate. Price a bundle above current AOV.
- **Promo framing:** promoting on a low-competition occasion can lift conversion up to 25%.
  Curiosity and "leak" framing works because it's rare; overusing it collapses it.
- **Sold-out scarcity:** keep visible sold-out items in a 10 to 30% band. Past ~30%,
  shoppers feel choice is threatened and conversion drops.
- **Amazon:** a strong listing converts ~10%; top sellers get 60 to 70% of sales from
  organic. The backend search-term field is 250 characters. Sequence: listing quality,
  then PPC, then feed PPC winners back into the listing.
- **Site speed:** a 6-second mobile load has 106% higher bounce chance vs 1 second; a 1 to
  3 second increase raises bounce probability 30%+.
- **Store trust signals:** ~75% of ecommerce sales are mobile; 88% trust online reviews as
  much as personal recommendations; 93% say visuals are key to purchasing.

### Optimize Amazon listings before you spend on PPC, performance + relevance, and mine PPC data for the backend search terms

## The strategy
On Amazon, organic ranking is driven by two levers, *performance* (conversion rate, imagery, reviews, price, inventory) and *relevance* (keywords). Top sellers get 60-70% of sales from organic, so fixing the listing before pouring money into PPC is what makes the ads pay off. Then PPC data becomes your keyword research engine.

## When to use it
Any brand selling on Amazon, before scaling Sponsored Products spend, or when ad spend is high but organic rank and conversion are stuck.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Optimize the listing for performance first:** conversion rate, high-quality imagery (7+ images + at least one in-use video), reviews, competitive price, and healthy inventory. Amazon listings convert ~10% when done well.
2. **Optimize for relevance with keywords.** Put primary keywords in the title (research via Helium 10, AMZScout, Ahrefs, Semrush, Keyword Planner). Write a 250-1,000-word description with ~5 bullets, no keyword stuffing.
3. **Fill the backend search-term field completely.** Use all 250 characters, and deliberately include high-value terms that are NOT already in your title (duplicating title terms wastes the field).
4. **Mine PPC for keywords.** Pull your highest-converting search terms from PPC/Sponsored Products data and feed them back into the title, bullets, and backend field.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Benchmarks: ~10% Amazon conversion for a strong listing, 60-70% organic sales share for top sellers, a 250-char backend search-term field.
- Sequence matters: listing quality → then PPC → then feed PPC winners back into the listing. Ads amplify a good listing; they can't fix a bad one.

## → Skill conversion note
Skill candidate: "Optimize my Amazon listing." Input = current title/bullets/images + PPC search-term report. Output = a rewritten title + bullets, a 250-char backend field of non-title terms, and an image/video gap list, ordered performance-first.

### Raise AOV with tested bundles and stacked promos, and only recommend products people actually buy together

## The strategy
Bundles and promo stacks lift average order value only when they're built from real co-purchase data and tested before the big moment. Most stores get cross-sells wrong, 52% recommend irrelevant products and actively *hurt* AOV, so relevance is the whole game.

## When to use it
DTC/ecommerce stores planning a peak season (Black Friday), building bundle SKUs, or fixing under-performing cross-sell widgets and cart recommendations.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Test Black Friday bundles months ahead.** Group items customers already co-purchase, validate demand now via *unpaid* channels, and pick bundles that convert with low returns *without* a discount. Then discount only the proven winner, keeping the bundle price above your current AOV.
2. **Stack complementary promos** to lift AOV: free shipping + buy-2, flash bundle + shipping threshold, BOGO + a $50 minimum, plus personalized recommendations at checkout.
3. **Make cross-sells contextually relevant.** Feature one highly-relevant product, avoid competing alternatives, use "Frequently Bought Together" / thematic labels, and recommend complementary necessities. 52% of sites recommend irrelevant products and hurt AOV, relevance, not quantity, drives attach rate.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Tactics: test bundles ahead + discount only the winner; stacked promos; the 52%-recommend-irrelevant stat behind Frequently-Bought-Together relevance.
- Complements the CRO upsell/cross-sell card (which covers checkout upsell *mechanics*); this card covers bundle *construction* and product selection.

## → Skill conversion note
Skill candidate: "Build a tested bundle." Input = co-purchase data + AOV. Output = 2-3 candidate bundles priced above AOV, a no-discount validation plan on unpaid channels, and a relevance filter for cross-sell recommendations.

### Cut cart abandonment by removing the top 5 checkout frictions and recovering the rest by email

## The strategy
70-80% of carts are abandoned (up to ~95% during COVID). Most abandonment traces to five predictable frictions at checkout, attack each one, then recover the leftovers with an automated abandoned-cart email.

## When to use it
Any ecommerce/DTC checkout with a visible drop-off between add-to-cart and purchase. Run alongside the Shopify store-conversion card.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Diagnose against the top 5 abandonment reasons (ranked):** (1) extra costs / shipping too high, (2) mandatory account creation, (3) slow delivery, (4) long/complicated checkout, (5) credit-card trust concerns.
2. **Fix shipping cost:** offer free shipping universally or at a reasonable threshold, or bake shipping into product pricing; highlight the offer with a Free Shipping Bar (Hextom).
3. **Fix forced accounts:** make account creation optional, offer guest checkout, request only email (phone only if essential). Nielsen Norman: higher interaction cost = fewer completions.
4. **Shorten checkout / one-click:** implement Fast, Bolt, or Shopify Payments + Shop Pay; optionally accept crypto for flexibility.
5. **Fix trust & payment options:** enable PayPal; add Affirm and Klarna for flexible payment plans; keep the checkout page clean and distraction-free (St. Frank's).
6. **Recover abandoned carts:** send an automated email with a cart link after a set delay (MoMA Design Store does this).

## Notes / caveats / examples
- The single biggest lever is usually shipping cost, it's the #1 stated reason.
- Every extra field or step is measurable interaction cost; defer non-essential asks.
- Keep recovery emails prompt but not spammy; a direct cart link removes re-navigation friction.

## → Skill conversion note
Becomes a "checkout friction audit" SKILL.md: scores a checkout against the 5 abandonment reasons, flags forced-account and hidden-shipping traps, and drafts the abandoned-cart recovery email.

### Sell the same discount better, leak it, wrap it in curiosity, and run it on an obscure holiday you can own

## The strategy
The *framing* of a promo often matters more than the discount depth. The same sale converts far better when it's teased as a "leak," obscured to trigger curiosity, or attached to a low-competition occasion you can own, because novelty and mystery cut through promo fatigue.

## When to use it
Any DTC/ecommerce brand running sales, especially email/SMS-driven promotions or the pre-window buildup before a big sale (Black Friday, launches).

## How to execute (steps)
1. **"Leak" the discount as creative framing.** Present a time-bound offer as an accidental or insider leak rather than a straight "20% off", Brooklinen used this to drive one of its best sales days of the year.
2. **Build curiosity sequences instead of straight promos.** Blur/obscure the sale items with playful copy (Chubbies), or run a daily-deal drip *before* the main event (Poo-Pourri ran 6 deals over 12 days) so anticipation compounds.
3. **Invent or adopt an obscure holiday.** Promoting on a low-competition occasion can lift promo conversion up to 25% (BarkBox on National Squirrel Appreciation Day, Jan 21; National Dog Day). Owning a niche "holiday" gives you a repeatable, uncrowded promo moment every year.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Real examples: Brooklinen "leak" sale; Chubbies blur + Poo-Pourri 6-deals-over-12-days; obscure-holiday +25% lift via BarkBox.
- Caveat: curiosity/leak framing works because it's rare, overusing it collapses it back into ordinary promo noise.

## → Skill conversion note
Skill candidate: "Reframe my promo." Input = the offer + calendar. Output = a leak/curiosity angle, a pre-window daily-deal drip schedule, and a shortlist of ownable obscure holidays matched to the brand.

### Print QR reorder codes on consumable packaging to capture repurchase at the moment the product runs out

## The strategy
The highest-intent repurchase moment for a consumable is when the customer physically runs out, but that moment usually happens away from your site, and the intent evaporates. A QR code printed on the packaging captures that intent exactly when it peaks, turning the product itself into a one-scan reorder channel.

## When to use it
Any consumable/replenishable DTC product: food and beverage, personal care, household goods, supplements. Especially where reorder friction (re-finding the site, re-adding to cart) suppresses repeat rate.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Print a QR reorder code on the packaging**, bottle, box, or label, that deep-links straight to a pre-filled reorder / subscribe flow.
2. **Place it where the customer looks when the product is nearly empty** (near the pump, the last-use surface, the base).
3. **Make the scan land on a one-tap reorder** (cart pre-filled, or a subscribe-and-save option), not the generic homepage.
4. **Extend the pattern** to any restock-at-home category: sunscreen, detergent, beverages, coffee, pet food.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Graza olive oil captures repurchase intent at the restock moment via an on-bottle QR code.
- Why it works: it collapses the reorder funnel to a single scan at peak intent, instead of hoping the customer remembers your URL later.

## → Skill conversion note
Skill candidate: "Design a packaging reorder loop." Input = product + packaging surfaces. Output = QR placement recommendation, the pre-filled reorder/subscribe destination, and a subscribe-and-save hook to attach.

### Win the shipping objection, show delivery dates on the PDP and use a Prime-style free-shipping subscription

## The strategy
Shipping is the single most common cart-killer (48% of US shoppers abandon over shipping cost). Two levers beyond "just offer free shipping": remove *delivery-date* ambiguity on the product page before the cart, and convert frequent buyers into a Prime-style free-shipping membership that trades lower AOV for much higher frequency.

## When to use it
Any Shopify/DTC store where shipping cost or unclear delivery timing is driving abandonment, or a repeat-purchase brand that could support a membership.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Show estimated delivery dates on the PDP,** not just at checkout. Ambiguous delivery timing kills conversion; a concrete "arrives by [date]" removes the doubt while the shopper is still deciding. On Shopify, add it via custom Liquid (Online Store → Themes → Customize).
2. **Offer a free-shipping subscription (Prime model).** A membership that bundles free shipping lifts revenue per customer ~34%, it trades lower per-order AOV for higher purchase frequency and loyalty.
3. **Attack the cost objection directly** where relevant (free-shipping threshold or baked-in pricing), 48% of US shoppers abandon carts over shipping cost.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- Benchmarks: free-shipping subscription +34% revenue/customer, 48% shipping-cost abandonment; delivery-date-on-PDP implemented via Shopify Liquid.
- Complements the cart-abandonment card (which ranks shipping cost as the #1 checkout friction), this card adds the *pre-cart* delivery-date fix and the *membership* monetization angle.

## → Skill conversion note
Skill candidate: "Fix my shipping conversion." Input = current shipping model + delivery times + repeat-purchase rate. Output = a PDP delivery-date implementation note, a free-shipping-threshold recommendation, and a go/no-go on a membership based on purchase frequency.

### Launch a Shopify store that converts, homepage, product pages, checkout, CRO tools, and site speed

## The strategy
Optimize every on-site surface of a Shopify store against the formula **Conversion = Desire − Labor − Confusion**. Average Shopify conversion is 1.6%; top 20% hit 3.5%+ and top 10% hit 5.1%+, the gap is copy, imagery, and on-site flow, not the theme.

## When to use it
Launching or auditing a Shopify/DTC store's homepage, product pages, and checkout. Pair with the cart-abandonment card for the checkout funnel.

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Domain & theme.** Use a custom domain (drop "myshopify.com"); prefer exact-match, else add verbs ("get/shop/try") or alt TLDs (.shop); avoid cute puns (hurts SEO). Premade conversion-optimized themes run $0, $350, copy/images/flow matter more than the theme. Use BuiltWith to see competitors' themes; test on mobile and desktop.
2. **Homepage above-the-fold.** Short meaningful header/subheader conveying the offer without scrolling; imagery of products in real-world use with people (originals beat stock, Burst for free stock); high-contrast CTA (usually "shop now"). Sliders work for discounts/limited-time/seasonal/new items. Keep the CTA above the fold, moving it below cuts conversions.
3. **Below the fold**, ordered by journey priority: best sellers, social proof (reviews/press/UGC/testimonials, place near CTAs to handle objections at the decision point), content (blog/video, safer here than ATF), brand info (features/ingredients/mission), footer (returns policy, exchanges).
4. **Product pages.** Desktop: image left, details/CTA right; mobile: image top, details below (verify mobile before launch). Title leads with benefits + SEO keywords + star rating, price, and pay options (Shop Pay/Apple Pay/PayPal). Description centers the user ("you can feel good about…"), uses expandable sections so text doesn't block the CTA. Images: bright, multiple contexts, action text overlays ("peel, fold, and GO!"), GIFs, WebP format; keep the key value prop visible during the carousel. Add reviews (Yotpo) and a "You May Also Like" section with per-item add-to-cart/Apple Pay. Offer "Subscribe & Save" for consumables (e.g. 10% off); group-discount apps like Skio pull in referral users and disincentivize cancellation.
5. **Checkout.** Strip all non-purchase links; focus CTA on "place order/check out"; ask only for necessary info. Test a subtle late-stage upsell (Amazon's checkout recommendations gave "a significant boost to revenue"), keep it light to avoid abandonment.
6. **Site speed.** Audit with Google PageSpeed Insights. Ranked fixes: (1) eliminate content, cut unused images/sections, find them with Hotjar heatmaps (removing an image beats optimizing it); (2) optimize images to WebP + right size; (3) reduce scripts/plugins; (4) lazy-load; (5) improve one page at a time. Benchmarks: a 6-second mobile load has 106% higher bounce chance vs. 1-second; a 1→3s increase raises bounce probability 30%+.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- **Navigation:** put categories directly in the navbar (avoid a single "Products" dropbox); mega menus for large catalogs, ordered by popularity. Low-AOV (<$50) simple products → info pages/blog in footer; higher-AOV/complex/mission-driven → info pages in navbar. Keep the cart visible/sticky and high-contrast (Mahabis' bright red cart). Category/listing pages also rank for broader queries (LimeSpot for recommendations).
- **CRO tools:** popups convert ~3% (top performers ~10%), trigger at ~60% of average page time, max one per session, no more than weekly per visitor. Urgency: low-stock indicators + countdown timers (a countdown lifted conversions "almost three times higher" vs. price-only), but don't stack every tactic or it reads pushy. Live chat: web-chat users are 2.8x more likely to convert and spend 60% more (Intercom/Olark/Drift), keep it subtle.
- **A/B test** one variable at a time (image/header/CTA/description) with Google Optimize (free, GA-integrated) → scale to Optimizely; requires Google Analytics for Shopify set up first.
- 93% of shoppers say visuals are key to purchasing; ~75% of ecommerce sales are mobile; 88% trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations.
- Examples: Brooklinen ($50k Kickstarter → ~$100M revenue; Yotpo + Klaviyo), Mosaic Foods (multi-section homepage), Kirrin Finch (slider ATF with people), Pure Cycles (minimal ATF copy), Outlier (unique layout), Harper Wilde (promo carousel).

### Keep 10-30% of your catalog visibly "sold out", scarcity that signals desirability without threatening choice

## The strategy
Sold-out labels are usually treated as a failure to hide. Used deliberately, they're a conversion asset: a visible band of sold-out items signals that the brand is desirable and its products are quality (other people bought them), and adds urgency to what's still available, as long as you don't overdo it.

## When to use it
DTC/ecommerce catalogs, especially fashion, limited drops, and brands wanting to manufacture desirability signals. Works best when some genuine scarcity exists (restocks, drops, seasonal SKUs).

## How to execute (steps)
1. **Show 10-30% of the visible catalog as "sold out"** rather than hiding or de-listing those items. The sold-out state does double duty: social proof (this sells) + urgency (buy the in-stock ones now).
2. **Stay under the 30% ceiling.** Past ~30% sold-out, shoppers feel their *choice* is threatened and satisfaction/conversion drops, scarcity flips from enticing to frustrating.
3. **Pair with restock signals** ("back in stock soon" / "notify me") so sold-out items also capture demand and emails instead of dead-ending.

## Notes / caveats / examples
- The 10-30% band and the >30% choice-threat ceiling come from research on the "sold-out effect".
- Caveat: this is a display/merchandising choice about *genuinely* limited items, faking sold-out states across a fully-stocked catalog is a trust risk and can backfire on returning customers.

## → Skill conversion note
Skill candidate: "Merchandise scarcity." Input = catalog + real stock status. Output = which items to keep visible as sold-out to hit the 10-30% band, plus a "notify me" capture on each.
